HEATSCAPE / User guideReturn to the system

Find a building. Understand its priority.

HEATSCAPE is a student prototype for energy-support investigation. It combines heat, neighbourhood economic context and building scale. It does not decide funding or measure indoor conditions.

Find

Search a building name, ID, address, SA1, suburb or SA2. Select a building to pin its score. Choose “See this building’s score and evidence” to open Priorities.

Priorities

Drag the H/I/B dividers; choose 50, 100 or 150 m and a shortlist size. Heat uses the fixed nine-date summary and historical branch. There is no heat-date selector. Inside each indicator has two sliders: recent/historical within H, and low income/rent-to-income within I. Drag either from 0% to 100%; the score updates on release. Reset restores H40/I40/B20 and 70/30 for both internal pairs. These are adjustable starting choices.

The list sits beside the controls. “Show consistent Top on map” isolates candidates shortlisted in every tested setting. “Clear filter” returns to the wider map. The same cohort and shortlist size are used in all tests; missing evidence is not failure.

Read a building’s evidence

Expand “H / I / B calculation tree” to see each source’s contribution. Expand “Building type, age & rental evidence” for CLUE matches and area rental benchmarks. Unknown fields remain unknown. Building age/type do not imply resident income. The rent/income proxy is a ratio of SA1 medians, not household rental stress.

Test settings and results

The default suite has 162 tests: six H/I/B choices × three extents × three recent/historical H blends × three low-income/rent-to-income I blends. Every combination is tested. Custom shares add to the relevant dimension and are included in the audit. Click a cell to apply its scenario. The initial Top 20% suite has 138 consistently selected buildings from a common 2,560-building cohort. Counts change with shortlist size. The live default ranks 2,561 buildings; it is not the same denominator as the complete-test cohort.

Layers and map

H and I map colours now show their full normalised composites (0–100), not raw temperatures or low-income percentages. Raw source values remain in the evidence. B shows building scale. The selected heat extent is filled: gold shows ground sun-exposed on all accepted dates, pale gold on some accepted dates. Gaps exclude mapped buildings or always-shaded ground. These geometric masks are not temperature colours or a cloud-valid pixel map. The dashed line is the outer extent.

Heat data quality

After selecting a building, open Priorities → Heat data quality. It shows the weakest coverage, area and effective-cell support, plus the largest three-cell weight share among contributing dates. Expand Compare all three extents for LST, score and rank at 50/100/150 m. Matched-date checks use the same dates at both extents.

100 m is the default; 150 m checks broader surroundings; 50 m is exploratory. A warning flags limited support, not an automatic score exclusion. The audit does not prove a building temperature is accurate.

About data → Heat quality shows the five-council audit and downloadable diagnostics. It also explains the daily weighted mean and the median across dates, with an expandable Swanston Central example. The median can hide peak heat; the tested settings do not establish temperature accuracy.

About data

Use the Heat sources, Income & housing and Building validation buttons. Read sources, periods, coverage, matching rules and assumptions. CLUE covers City of Melbourne only. Homes Victoria rents are suburb/council benchmarks, not building rents. Every added source is listed.

Export

Shortlist exports include both internal shares, H/I subcomponents, data periods, CLUE type/years, the area cost-to-income proxy and quality flags. Export list saves stability membership and ranks; Export test table saves all scenarios and the selected building’s results. Read the equations and limitations.

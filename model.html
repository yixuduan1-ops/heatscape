HEATSCAPE / MODELReturn to the system ↗

From dated heat evidence to H, I and B.

Choose a Landsat observation, the median of valid dates, or historical CSIRO surface temperature in Priorities. H is a spatial surface-temperature statistic. It is not indoor heat, air temperature, health risk or a predicted energy saving.

1. Select and check the heat source

Nine USGS Collection 2 Level-2 observations run from 29 December 2025 to 23 February 2026. For ST_B10, °C = DN × 0.00341802 + 149 − 273.15; each scene’s scale and offset are checked in its MTL file. QA_PIXEL bits 0–5 exclude fill, dilated cloud, cirrus, cloud, cloud shadow and snow. Missing ST is rejected. ST_QA × 0.01 K describes pixel uncertainty; it is reported separately. QA_RADSAT and ST_CDIST were not supplied.

Dates are reprojected by nearest neighbour to the common 30 m GDA2020 / MGA zone 55 grid. The roughly 100 m native thermal information is not sharpened by reprojection or geometric masks. About data lists source files, checksums, scene angles and coverage for each council and extent.

2. Build the observation-time sampling area

Let F be the target footprint and R be 50, 100 or 150 metres. Begin with buffer(F,R) minus F. Exclude every mapped building footprint and all building shadows reaching that area, including the target’s self-shadow. Overlapping shadows are unioned once. Hidden context buildings still participate.

Shadow length = height / tan(solar elevation)
Shadow bearing = (solar azimuth + 180°) mod 360°
A(date,R) = exterior buffer − mapped footprints − observation-time shadows

Sun angles come from each scene’s metadata. True bearing at the study centre is transformed to the projected grid. A building footprint is swept along the height-scaled shadow displacement. Search distance includes the largest possible shadow reach, so buildings outside R can cast shade into it.

Flat ground and vertical extrusions are assumptions. Trees, terrain, roof forms, facades and buildings outside the inventory are not modelled. Many heights are estimated; About data records their source and official building-use checks. Current building geometry is applied to historical imagery, so dates do not match perfectly.

3. Calculate surrounding surface heat

w(x) = exp(−2[d(x,F)/R]²)
T(date,R) = Σ [a × w × valid source temperature] / Σ [a × w over valid ground]

A 2.5 m midpoint grid approximates integration over A. Every sample retains its source-cell temperature; it is not a 2.5 m thermal measurement. Valid-area fraction and valid-weight fraction must both reach 80%, with at least 3 valid resampled grid cells. A smaller or patchier area can have weak thermal support even when these screening rules pass. The UI reports cells, coverage, area and area divided by 10,000 m²; this last value is not an independent-pixel count.

The multi-date option takes the median of valid T(date,R) values and requires at least 3 dates. Its support values are minima across contributing dates. Different buildings may use different dates. It describes these supplied observations, not a whole summer or temperature trend.

CSIRO 2018–19 LST is converted from Kelvin to °C and sampled over open ground at each R without one dated shadow mask. Its Melbourne source dates are 28 January and 24 March 2019. The published retrieval method differs from USGS Collection 2. CSIRO UHI, Victorian 2018 Mesh Block heat and 1981–2010 air-temperature normals are additional evidence, not extra H/I/B weights.

4. Put H, I and B on relative scales

Percentile = (average ascending rank − 0.5) / N
B = percentile(mean(height percentile, footprint-area percentile))

I, height, area and B use the fixed candidates with valid income, positive height and footprint area. H uses valid temperatures from that reference population for the chosen source and extent. Ties share a midrank. Between reference knots, interpolation is linear; values beyond the reference range clamp to an endpoint. Council, map and shortlist filters do not refit the curves.

I is the 2021 SA1 share of households below A$650 per week among households with known income, including negative and nil income. Partial or unstated income is excluded. An interior footprint point assigns the census area. This is neighbourhood context, not measured resident income. B represents scale, not measured dwelling count or energy use.

5. Weight, rank and shortlist

Score = 100 × (wH × H + wI × I + wB × B)
wH + wI + wB = 1; default 0.4 / 0.4 / 0.2

All candidates use the same chosen weights. H and I evidence are required; B evidence is required when its weight is nonzero. Rank valid scores descending. Top percentage requests ceil(N × percentage /100); Top number requests the chosen count, capped at N. Exact score ties at the cutoff are included. Missing evidence is never zero need.

6. Test ranking stability

Six weight sets × three extents gives 18 tests for the selected source. Turning on Test all 9 Landsat dates gives 162 tests; custom weights add three tests per date. Historical and median sources are not inserted into the nine-date suite.

Every test ranks the same complete-evidence cohort and uses the same shortlist size. A missing input excludes that building from all comparative test ranks and shows its valid-test count separately. The current live rank still uses its own source’s eligible sample, so it can differ from its test rank. “All tested settings” is limited to these tested combinations, not every possible setting or proof of robustness. A small complete-case cohort can be spatially biased.

HEATSCAPE / MODELReturn to the system ↗

From geometry to H, I and B.

The active heat model uses sun-exposed open ground. The switch in Priorities restores the original exterior-buffer model. Both models are calculated for every candidate with the same policy weights. This page documents the implemented equations, rather than a physical cooling model.

The °C difference is a difference between spatial summaries of the same Landsat image. It is not a prediction of cooling, indoor temperature, air temperature or retrofit benefit. Excluding shade does not guarantee a hotter or cooler mean.

1. Building geometry and sunlight

Let Fᵢ be the target footprint, Fⱼ a surrounding footprint, hⱼ its height, and R = 50, 100 or 150 m. Coordinates and distances are in GDA2020 / MGA zone 55 metres. Every available context building participates, including those hidden on the map.

gᵢⱼ = min ‖x − y‖, x ∈ Fᵢ, y ∈ Fⱼ
Lⱼ = hⱼ / tan(α)
vⱼ = −Lⱼ (sin β, cos β)   [east, north]
Sⱼ = Fⱼ ⊕ { t vⱼ : 0 ≤ t ≤ 1 }

g is the minimum footprint gap. α is solar elevation and β is azimuth clockwise from true north. Shadows are the swept footprint, not a circle around a centroid. The true bearing is transformed to the projected grid. Actual positions encode both gap and direction: proximity alone does not imply shade. Near-zero gaps may reflect shared podiums or overlapping footprints rather than tower clearance.

For the image: 7 January 2026, 11:03:46 AEDT; α = 56.65410279°, β = 73.04185990°. L ≈ 0.6580h; shadows point approximately 253.04°. A 100 m building projects roughly 65.8 m on flat ground. Tall buildings outside R can still shade the area and are included in the shadow search.

2. The changed heat sampling area

Aᵢ⁰(R) = buffer(Fᵢ, R) ∖ Fᵢ
Gᵢ(R) = Aᵢ⁰(R) ∖ ⋃ⱼ Fⱼ
Aᵢ¹(R) = Gᵢ(R) ∖ ⋃ⱼ Sⱼ
retained area (%) = 100 |Aᵢ¹| / |Aᵢ⁰|

Original model: A⁰. Sun-exposed model: A¹, which excludes all building footprints and both neighbouring and self-shadows. This makes irregular edges and holes. Overlapping shadows are counted once. The mask is binary: shaded/built locations have zero weight; included ground uses the distance curve below.

dᵢ(x) = min ‖x − y‖, y ∈ Fᵢ
kᵢ(x;R) = exp[−2(dᵢ(x)/R)²]
wᵢₚᵐ = ∫[Pₚ ∩ Aᵢᵐ] kᵢ(x;R) dx
Tᵢᵐ = Σₚ(valid) wᵢₚᵐ LSTₚ / Σₚ(valid) wᵢₚᵐ   (m = 0,1)

At the footprint edge the distance weight is 1; at R it is exp(−2) ≈ 0.1353. This is a spatial emphasis rule, not a heat-transfer law. Sun-exposed integration uses 2.5 m midpoint quadrature while retaining the source pixel temperature. It does not create 2.5 m thermal measurements. Display boundaries are simplified by 0.15 m; numerical masks are not.

LSTₚ(°C) = DNₚ × 0.00341802 + 149 − 273.15
valid-area coverage = valid included area / included area
valid-weight coverage = valid included weight / included weight

Fill and QA_PIXEL cloud, cirrus, cloud shadow and snow are screened. Both coverage measures must be at least 80%; the sun-exposed mean additionally needs at least three valid source grid cells. No valid evidence means unavailable, not zero heat. Native thermal resolution is approximately 100 m, resampled to 30 m; adjacent cells and the two compared areas may share thermal information.

3. The percentile response curves

PX(x) = [n(X < x) + ½ n(X = x)] / N
Between reference knots (xₖ,pₖ), (xₖ₊₁,pₖ₊₁):
P(x) = pₖ + (pₖ₊₁−pₖ)(x−xₖ)/(xₖ₊₁−xₖ)

These empirical, piecewise-linear curves are generated from the actual study data; no logistic or polynomial curve is fitted. Values outside the reference range clamp to the nearest endpoint. Exact ties share a midrank. The reference cohort has valid original 100 m heat, income, height and area. Sun-exposed H uses the sun-exposed temperatures of that cohort at the chosen radius. Council and shortlist filters never refit these curves.

Hᵢᵐ = 100 PTᵐ(R)(Tᵢᵐ)
qᵢ = 100 × households below A$650/week / households with known income
Iᵢ = 100 Pq(qᵢ)

Income is assigned from the Census 2021 SA1 containing the footprint interior point. Negative and nil income are included; partial or unstated income is excluded. It is neighbourhood context, not the income of this building’s residents.

4. Building scale

uᵢ = Pheight(hᵢ),   vᵢ = Parea(|Fᵢ|)
zᵢ = (uᵢ + vᵢ)/2
Bᵢ = 100 Pz(zᵢ)

B is a policy preference for scale. It does not measure occupants, energy use or actual need. Height enters both shadow geometry and scale, but is not added as a separate extra score. I and B remain identical when the heat-model switch changes.

5. Weights, ranks and differences

wB = b/100,   a = balance/100
wH = (1−wB)a,   wI = (1−wB)(1−a)
Scoreᵢᵐ = wHHᵢᵐ + wIIᵢ + wBBᵢ
rankᵢᵐ = 1 + n(Scoreⱼᵐ > Scoreᵢᵐ)
heat rankᵢᵐ = 1 + n(Tⱼᵐ > Tᵢᵐ)

b ranges from 0–40 and balance from 0–100. Defaults give H 40%, I 40%, B 20%. Higher values rank first. Competition ranks preserve exact ties (1, 1, 3). Heat ranks use heat-valid candidates; overall ranks require the scoring inputs. Eligible counts are displayed for both models.

ΔTᵢ = Tᵢ¹ − Tᵢ⁰
ΔHᵢ = Hᵢ¹ − Hᵢ⁰
ΔScoreᵢ = Scoreᵢ¹ − Scoreᵢ⁰ = wHΔHᵢ
rank improvement = rankᵢ⁰ − rankᵢ¹

Positive rank improvement means moving closer to rank 1. A higher temperature does not always mean a higher percentile after switching, because the entire heat reference distribution changes. All buildings are recalculated together using identical policy weights.

Top percentage: K = ceil(N × p/100)
Top count: K = min(N, requested count)
Selectedᵢ = 1[Scoreᵢ ≥ score at position K]
Spatial consistency = selected extents / eligible extents, R ∈ {50,100,150}

Score ties at the cutoff are included. Spatial consistency uses the active heat mode and current policy weights; it is not a probability. H ≥ 80 and I ≥ 80 marks jointly high heat and disadvantage, not a separate indicator or score bonus.

6. Shadow context and limitations

Neighbour shade (%) = 100 |Gᵢ ∩ ⋃ⱼ≠ᵢ Sⱼ| / |Gᵢ|
All shade (%) = 100 |Gᵢ ∩ ⋃ⱼ Sⱼ| / |Gᵢ|
Open ground (%) = 100 |Gᵢ| / |Aᵢ⁰|

Shade percentages and footprint gaps explain the changed geometry; they are not separately added to H. The geometric model assumes flat ground and vertical extruded footprints at one observation time. Heights may be estimated; missing heights cannot cast modelled shadows. Trees, facade/roof temperatures, materials, wind, reflected radiation and thermal storage are not modelled. This is not a roof/facade shading model. Source dates differ, and buildings outside the study data can cast missing shadows. Rankings are not validated against indoor conditions.

A physical temperature model would require additional meteorological forcing, surface properties and validation. This model intentionally answers a narrower question: how does sampling only modelled sun-exposed ground change the observed-LST summary and relative shortlist?

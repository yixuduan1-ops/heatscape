HEATSCAPE / ModelReturn to the system

One integrated H–I–B model

The default combines recent heat (70%) with a historical temperature index (30%). I represents economic pressure in a census neighbourhood; B represents building scale. Scores support further investigation, not automatic energy-support eligibility.

1. Recent heat from nine dates

USGS Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 Level-2 observations: 29 Dec 2025; 6, 7, 23 and 30 Jan 2026; 7, 15, 16 and 23 Feb 2026. Source files, dates, solar angles, hashes and five-council coverage remain in About data. The app has no date/source selector.

ST_B10 × 0.00341802 + 149 − 273.15 converts to °C (checked against each MTL). QA_PIXEL bits 0–5 exclude fill, dilated cloud, cirrus, cloud, cloud shadow and snow. Non-positive/missing ST is excluded; water is retained. ST_QA × 0.01 K reports pixel uncertainty, not uncertainty of a building mean. QA_RADSAT and ST_CDIST were not supplied.

Nearest-neighbour reprojection uses a common 30 m MGA55 grid. Native thermal resolution remains roughly 100 m. For each date, heights and that scene’s solar angles project vertical building shadows onto flat ground. A footprint buffer excludes all mapped footprints and the union of modelled shadows. Flat ground, incomplete context geometry and estimated heights limit this approximation.

L(d,R) = Σ [valid area × exp(−2(distance/R)²) × pixel temperature] / Σ [valid area × exp(−2(distance/R)²)]
R = 50, 100 or 150 m
M(R) = median of L(d,R) over valid dates; require at least 3 dates.

Area integration uses 2.5 m quadrature. Each date requires at least 80% valid sampled area, 80% valid weight and 3 valid resampled grid cells. A small buffer is not necessarily resolved by the thermal sensor. Multi-day support displays minima over contributing dates. Different buildings may contribute different dates; the median is not a seasonal mean, continuous record or indoor temperature.

2. H: temperature evidence only

Recent L: percentile of the multi-day median surface-temperature statistic at the chosen extent.

Historical T: equal mean of three source percentiles: CSIRO 2018–19 UHI, Victoria 2018 UHI and Climate Victoria summer air-temperature normals.

T = [P(CSIRO UHI) + P(Victoria UHI) + P(Climate summer)] / 3
H = aL + (1 − a)T; initial a = 0.70, adjustable 0–1

CSIRO UHI is area/distance-weighted over open ground at each extent; the historical mosaic has no single dated shadow mask. Its LST is source evidence behind the UHI and is not counted separately. Victoria UHI uses the unique 2016 Mesh Block containing the footprint interior point. Climate uses the mean of December, January and February 1981–2010 maximum-air-temperature normals at the source grid point (~250 m), then a percentile.

The different products are normalised separately; their raw Celsius temperatures are never averaged together. All three historical components must be available. The initial 70/30 H blend and equal historical source shares are prototype assumptions, not fitted impact coefficients. The internal H slider adjusts the recent/historical pair; the internal I slider adjusts low-income/rent-to-income. Each pair sums to 100%. A missing source with zero selected share does not prevent the live score, but the cross-setting audit still requires all tested evidence. Related UHI products are correlated; percentiles may amplify a small climate gradient. The historical index is context, not current weather, a trend, indoor heat or measured energy need.

The peer report informed discovery of official historical heat sources. Its full composite, SA1 risk bins and exact final map are not reproduced.

Swanston Central at 100 m: median LST 35.977°C from 6 valid dates → recent percentile 60.346. Historical source percentiles are 9.568, 44.535 and 73.955 → mean 42.686. H = 0.70×60.346 + 0.30×42.686 = 55.048 /100. Default H/I/B weighting then gives 81.5 /100, rank 54 of 2,561.

3. I calculation tree

Income: SA1 households below A$650/week divided by households with known income (ABS 2021 G33). Negative/nil income is included; partial/unstated income excluded.

Cost-to-income proxy: same-year SA1 median weekly rent divided by median weekly household income (ABS 2021 G02).

I = b × P(low-income share) + (1 − b) × P(area median rent / area median household income)
Initial b = 0.70, adjustable 0–1

The ratio uses separate area medians and the income median includes both renters and owners. It is not a household payment ratio, an ABS rental-stress rate or evidence that a resident spends a given share on rent. Both components remain shared within an SA1. Non-positive/missing medians or missing low-income share prevent a complete I; there is no fabricated building-level income.

CLUE use, construction/refurbishment year and dwelling records are separate building evidence. Neither building age nor student/commercial accommodation adds an assumed income penalty. Homes Victoria September 2025 suburb/LGA rent tables are context only: current rents are not divided by 2021 incomes. Bedrooms are not assigned to a building, and median rents are not averaged across bedroom categories.

4. CLUE and rental matching

CLUE 2024 covers City of Melbourne only. Property ID links dwelling records; unique point-in-footprint associations supply display attributes. There are 320 candidate associations, 269 unambiguous construction years and 40 refurbishment years. Distinct uses/years remain conflicts; shared/proximity matches stay in the validation panel. A missing refurbishment date does not mean never refurbished, and a refurbishment is not proof of an energy upgrade.

Recorded types: 239 residential apartments, 18 student accommodation, 10 commercial accommodation, 10 mixed/conflicting uses, 43 other uses and 2,253 without a unique CLUE match. Commercial accommodation is not automatically serviced apartments. These classifications are review evidence, not a verified apartment inventory or automatic exclusion.

Homes Victoria September 2025 tables link 2,526 candidates to named suburb groups and all 2,573 to council-level benchmarks. Suburb values are moving annual medians; council values are quarterly medians. Student housing and rooming houses are excluded by that source. Suppressed values and uncertain matches remain unavailable. Project-level UDP dates/counts are never silently substituted for building-level attributes.

5. Normalisation, B and the final score

Equations use P in the 0–1 range; displayed H, I and B multiply those values by 100. P is a relative midrank percentile, linearly interpolated between distinct source values. Reference candidates have valid income, height and footprint (2,571); each component uses its available values within that fixed reference universe. Thus reference curves do not move with UI filters or chosen weights. Heat references differ by extent. Composite H and I combine these normalised components and are not themselves re-ranked.

B = P[(P(height) + P(footprint area)) / 2]
Score = 100 × (wH·H + wI·I + wB·B)
Default shares = 0.40 / 0.40 / 0.20

All weights are exploratory choices, not fitted or validated impact coefficients. All candidates receive the same shares. A missing required component prevents scoring; a component with zero outer weight need not be present. B does not represent verified dwelling counts or energy demand. Of 2,573 candidates, 1,920 use estimated height values; these also affect shadows.

At the default 100 m / 70:30 heat blend, 2,561 candidates score: 2 lack usable income and 10 lack climate context. Swanston Central: H 55.0, I 98.8, B 99.6; score 81.5, rank 54.

6. What the 162 tests mean

Six outer H/I/B choices (40/40/20, 60/20/20, 20/60/20, 30/30/40, 50/50/0, exact thirds) × three extents (50/100/150 m) × three internal H blends (60/40, 70/30, 80/20 recent/historical) × three internal I blends (60/40, 70/30, 80/20 low income/rent-to-income). Every combination is evaluated.

Any custom outer shares or internal H/I shares are appended to their respective dimension, and the full combination set is recalculated. One custom internal share produces 216 tests; custom shares in both H and I produce 288 tests. Clicking a cell applies all settings. Reset restores outer 40/40/20 and internal 70/30 for H and I. The nine source observations remain fixed.

Every default test ranks the same 2,560 candidates with evidence for the full suite. Top 20% requests 512 positions; cutoff ties are included. At those settings, 138 buildings enter every tested shortlist. Swanston Central enters 153/162, rank 9–671. The live 100 m ranking has 2,561 eligible candidates and requests 513 positions; its default score stays 81.5, rank 54. Filters do not recalculate ranks.

Missing tests are not failed selections: their comparative rank/count stays unavailable. Stability is not validation, a probability or a guarantee across untested settings. Inspect indoor conditions, actual use, occupant needs, tenure, equipment and retrofit feasibility before intervention.

7. Heat data quality and sampling distances

100 m remains the default screening extent; 150 m checks broader surroundings, and 50 m is explicitly exploratory. The available source resolution cannot establish a universal optimal radius. Larger buffers pool more local environments rather than creating a better building-temperature measurement.

Using exact unsimplified per-date sampling geometry, the audit sums quadrature weights within each valid 30 m cell, then normalises them to p. Effective grid cells = 1/Σp²; top-three share is the sum of the three largest p values. These describe weight concentration, not independent native thermal observations. USGS TIRS thermal resolution is approximately 100 m; delivered 30 m cells are resampled.

Each building uses minima (cells, area, effective cells) or maxima (top-three share) over its accepted dates. Table medians are across buildings with >=3 valid dates at that extent. Paired differences compare the same observation dates, requiring >=3 paired dates.

The 50% top-three share and 10,000 m² geometric area are transparent review flags, not validated accuracy thresholds and not new score exclusions. 10,000 m² equals one 100 m square in area, not one independent thermal footprint.

The quality panel shows accepted dates, minimum cell count, minimum valid-area/weight coverage, retained sunlit area, minimum effective cells and maximum top-three share. Extent comparisons keep current weights fixed. Matched-date temperature differences use only dates accepted at both extents, to avoid confusing cloud/date availability with a distance effect.
